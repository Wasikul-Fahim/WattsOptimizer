WattsOptimizer — "Who Stays On?"
Slide points for the HackApex presentation
==========================================================================


SLIDE 1 — Title
---------------
- WattsOptimizer: "Who Stays On?"
- Fair, rule-safe generator rosters for markets during load shedding
- Team name / members
- One line: "We decide, hour by hour, which shops get generator power, with no rule broken
  and the most value served across the whole day."


SLIDE 2 — The Problem (the story)
---------------------------------
- Shapla Shopping Complex, Mirpur. DESCO load shedding: 12–4 PM and 5–8 PM, so 7 hours
  without grid power
- One 20 kW generator, but the shops together want nearly 40 kW. Turn them all on and it trips.
- Only 17 litres of diesel, and the tanker doesn't come before Saturday
- The pharmacy (insulin fridge) and the ATM booth must be on every hour
- 10 other shops all want power, and each one's busy hours are different
  (photocopy in the morning, mobile shop and restaurant in the evening, salon 1–4 PM...)
- Last year it was done with a pencil: the diesel ran out at 6:30 PM and the first floor went
  dark in the evening rush


SLIDE 3 — Why This Is Hard
--------------------------
- Fuel is charged on the COMBINED load per hour (a table lookup, rounded up to the next level),
  not per shop. Adding one small shop can move the whole hour up a fuel level.
- No normal shop may be dark more than 2 outage hours in a row (grid hours reset the count)
- Capacity limit in every hour, and a hard diesel budget for the whole day
- Litres must be exact to the hundredth, with no rounding tolerance
- The obvious approach (power the most valuable shops each hour) FAILS:
  it burns the diesel at noon and leaves the evening dark


SLIDE 4 — Constraints at a Glance (the rules)
---------------------------------------------
- Capacity: powered kW ≤ generator capacity, every outage hour
- Fuel: sum of per-hour fuel ≤ budget, with fuel looked up once per hour on the combined load
- Mandatory: mandatory shops are on in every outage hour
- Dark runs: no normal shop dark > max_consecutive_dark hours in a row
- Objective: maximize the total value served
- If the rules can't all hold: say "infeasible" and name the rule, shops and hours
- Bad input: "invalid_input" naming the exact field (HTTP 400)


SLIDE 5 — Our Solution
----------------------
- A web app plus a public API: POST /api/roster (input JSON in, roster JSON out)
- It plans the WHOLE DAY AT ONCE instead of hour by hour
- It returns, for each outage hour, the powered and dark shops, the load, the fuel and the
  running total
- Every dark shop gets a truthful one-line reason, with real numbers
- It runs fully offline with one start command: python app.py (port 8000)


SLIDE 6 — How It Works: the Optimizer
-------------------------------------
- Google OR-Tools CP-SAT: an exact integer optimization model
- One yes/no decision per shop per outage hour (at most 12 × 10 = 120 decisions)
- Each rule maps 1:1 to a constraint, so it is easy to explain:
    * Capacity:   Σ kw · on ≤ capacity          (each hour)
    * Fuel:       pick exactly one fuel level per hour; total litres ≤ budget
    * Mandatory:  on = 1
    * Dark runs:  every window of (D+1) hours with no grid hour must have ≥ 1 "on"
    * Objective:  maximize value; ties go to the roster that burns less diesel
- PROVEN optimal in about 0.1 seconds for twelve shops (limit is 10 s)
- Fuel is held in integer centilitres, so 8.00 vs 8.00 L never falls to floating-point noise
- Safety net: if OR-Tools is missing or times out (5 s), a greedy fallback runs and is
  clearly labelled "best-effort, not proven optimal"


SLIDE 7 — Explainable Decisions (every ✗ has a reason)
-----------------------------------------------------
- Key insight: in an optimal roster, a dark shop with value is ALWAYS blocked by capacity or
  fuel. Otherwise switching it on would be allowed and better.
- Reason types:
    * "needs 25 kW, generator carries 20 kW"
    * "worth 0 at 14:00; left off to save diesel"
    * "capacity: 18 kW on + 4 kW = 22 kW > 20 kW"
    * "fuel: +4 kW lifts 12:00 from 14 kW to 18 kW (+0.80 L); day total would be
       17.20 L > 17.00 L — diesel kept for mobile_shop at 18:00 (value 8),
       restaurant at 19:00 (value 8)"
- Reasons are worked out from the final roster after solving, so they are always true
- Infeasible inputs are diagnosed: which rule, which shops, which hours, plus a labelled
  best-effort roster


SLIDE 8 — Results
-----------------
- Brief's worked example: value 84, fuel 8.00 L of 8.00 L (matches the official answer)
- Twelve-shop sample: value 186, fuel 16.40 L of 17.00 L, every rule satisfied
- Minute-150 update (DESCO extends the outage): re-run and recompute in under a second
- Proven optimal, not just "good enough"


SLIDE 9 — Bug We Found and Fixed (evidence)
-------------------------------------------
- Version 1 was a greedy, hour-by-hour solver
- On the twelve-shop sample it burned 2.80 L in each of the first 6 outage hours
  (16.80 L), so only 0.20 L was left at 19:00
- Result: at 19:00 even the pharmacy and ATM were dark.
  Our checker found 7 violations: 2 mandatory-shop breaks and 5 dark-run breaks
- Fix: one whole-day CP-SAT model. Same input: 186 value (vs 183), 16.40 L,
  zero violations
- This shows the brief's warning in practice: you must plan the day as a whole


SLIDE 10 — Boundary Case (evidence)
-----------------------------------
- Load exactly equals capacity (10 kW of 10 kW) and fuel exactly equals budget (8.00 L of 8.00 L)
- Both rules say "≤", so this roster must be ACCEPTED
- Integer centilitre arithmetic keeps it exact
- Other side of the edge (tested): budget 7.99 L makes the solver drop below 84 rather than
  overspend by 0.01 L

- Click any ✗ to see its reason

SLIDE 13 — Architecture
-----------------------
- Request flow:
    1. schema.py       strict validation (400 naming the bad field)
    2. solver.py       CP-SAT whole-day model (greedy fallback)
    3. reasons.py      a truthful reason for every dark cell
    4. diagnose.py     names the conflict if infeasible
    5. output.py       totals recomputed from the roster, never trusted from the solver
- Stack: Python, FastAPI + uvicorn, OR-Tools, SQLite (logins only), React frontend
- validate_output.py: an independent rule checker that can verify any output file
- Decision logic is in ONE file: whostayson/solver.py (found in under 2 minutes)


SLIDE 14 — Quality and Security
-------------------------------
- 153 automated tests, all passing: schema, solver vs brute force, reasons truthfulness,
  diagnosis, API, auth, security
- Solver checked against brute force on small inputs, so we know it is truly optimal
- Security: strict validation, 64 KB body cap, Content-Security-Policy, no inline scripts,
  parameterised SQL, PBKDF2 password hashes, signed HttpOnly SameSite cookies,
  login throttling, no outbound network calls
- Offline install from vendored wheels, no internet needed at runtime

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
LIKELY JUDGE QUESTIONS (have answers ready, since anyone may be asked)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Why not greedy?
A: It can't see the evening. Our evidence shows it ran dry by 19:00 and broke 7 rules.

Q: How do you know it's optimal?
A: CP-SAT proves optimality, and our tests compare it against brute force on small inputs.

Q: How is fuel modelled?
A: One level indicator per hour (plus a free "0 kW" level). Exactly one is chosen, the load
   must fit inside that level's range, and the sum of litres must be ≤ the budget. All values
   are in centilitres.

Q: How are dark runs enforced?
A: Every window of D+1 consecutive outage hours (no grid hour inside) must contain at least
   one "on" hour.

Q: What if no valid roster exists?
A: status "infeasible". diagnose.py checks mandatory load vs capacity, then mandatory fuel vs
   budget, then relaxes the dark-run rule to name the conflicting shops and hours. It also
   attaches a labelled best-effort roster.

Q: Are the reasons ever wrong?
A: They are derived from the final roster and tested for truthfulness. If no simple reason
   applies, we re-solve with that cell forced on and report the real value trade-off.

Q: What if OR-Tools fails?
A: The greedy fallback runs, is labelled "solver": "greedy", and is never presented as optimal.

Q: Ties between equal-value rosters?
A: The tie-break prefers less diesel, so the result is deterministic.
